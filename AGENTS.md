# QuantumDaily project rules

Version: `2026-11-16-r1`. The English text is authoritative. Keep [the Chinese companion](AGENTS.zh-CN.md) aligned when changing these project rules.

## 1. Audience and scope

Write in Simplified Chinese for an engineer learning general quantum algorithms, simulating them and implementing them on hardware. Cover algorithms, classical competitors, hardware access and software, with room for field trends and public career or team changes. Place contributions on the academia–industry and science–engineering axes where that helps explain them. Choose tools to fit the evidence and reproduction task.

## 2. Sources and dates

For every brief and weekly, consult arXiv quant-ph, Quantum and the institutional watchlist in the [README](README.md#source-watchlist). Search official news, labs and papers, including dated submissions by affiliated authors. Search the Greater Bay Area across Guangdong, Hong Kong and Macao. Treat press syndication as a lead. Cite the originating paper, data, research group, company or filing beside the claim. Separate the dates of publication, revision, announcement, experiment and effective appointment where they differ. Record a material search gap once. Select stories on evidence and relevance; institutions have no article quota.

## 3. Direct scientific prose

Start with the actor, the result and what was measured or proved. Give each paragraph one task. Explain the operation that connects a method to its result. Specify the comparison population, baseline, units, precision and resource budget next to a number. Distinguish an author's finding, a local check, an engineering estimate and a trend when their evidence differs.

Delete sentences that answer an imagined objection or merely announce caution. Replace them with the condition that affects the result or the decision. For example, “eight jobs reached the reference loss in the eight-qubit run” gives a reader a testable scope. Preserve substantive negation in a failed test, impossibility result or discrepancy between sources. Explain each limitation once, where it matters. After fact-checking, read the complete issue for repeated conclusions, empty transitions and unexplained terms.

## 4. Learning, verification and cost

For the priority result, identify the smallest useful problem, reference answer, metric and route to hardware. Count the resources the method actually consumes, including sampling, compilation, connectivity or classical post-processing as applicable. If running a small local check, state the inputs and acceptance criterion. Report what ran and keep planned experiments in the action list. Paid hardware and large computations require separate authorization. In people coverage, name the role, institution, date and source of the change.

## 5. Publication

Follow the [automatic publication workflow](README.md#automatic-publication). Check remote main and the previous successful issue of the same type. Keep the brief and weekly windows independent. Preserve issue history when revising a published article and identify any later source check separately from its original cutoff. Build from Markdown, check the article and archive index, then confirm the GitHub files, Pages deployment and public URLs. Use PROJECT_SUMMARY.md for current state and TASK_LOG.md for milestones and recovery.
